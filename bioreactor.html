This is the molecule the whole plant exists to make: two heavy and two light chains in a Y shape. The arms (Fab) grab the drug target; the stem (Fc) is what Protein A grabs on the next page. CHO cells are used because, like human cells, they attach sugar chains (glycans) to the Fc, which the drug needs to work properly in the body.

Assumptions and limits

Batch only. Nothing is added after day 0. Real antibody plants run fed-batch (feeding glucose and nutrients daily) for 10–14 days, which is how they reach several g/L.

Glucose is the only food. Real cells also run out of glutamine and make lactate and ammonia, which slow growth. Those are left out.

No oxygen controller. Real bioreactors hold dissolved O₂ at a set point (often 30–50%) by adding pure oxygen. Here kLa is fixed, so you can see what happens without control.

One productivity number. The production clone's antibody per cell per day was fitted to one published titer (1 g/L). Real productivity changes with temperature, pH and culture age.

Numerics. RK4 with a step under 0.2/kLa hours. Tests check exact exponential growth, the yield balance, the oxygen steady state, and that halving the step changes the titer by less than 0.1%.

Words used on this page
CHO cells
Chinese hamster ovary cells. The workhorse cells for making antibody drugs.
Batch culture
Fill the tank once and let it run, like baking bread with only the dough you started with.
Viable cells
Living cells. Dead cells stop making antibody.
Monod equation
μ = μmax S/(Ks + S). Growth speeds up with food, then levels off, like a buffet line that can only move so fast.
Doubling time
Time for the cell count to double: td = ln 2 / μ.
Yield YX/S
Cells made per amount of glucose eaten.
kLa
Volumetric mass transfer coefficient. How fast oxygen crosses from bubbles into the liquid. Think of the width of a doorway.
C*
The most O₂ the liquid can hold when it is in contact with air. The "full tank" level.
OTR / OUR
Oxygen transfer rate (supply from bubbles) and oxygen uptake rate (demand from cells), both in mmol per L per hour.
Titer
Antibody concentration in the broth, g/L. The next steps purify it.
Luedeking–Piret
dP/dt = α dX/dt + β X: some product comes with growth (α), some just from being alive (β).
ODE, RK4
An equation for a rate of change, and a method that solves it in small steps, checking the slope four times per step.
