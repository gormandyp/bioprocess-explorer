Protein A grips the antibody's Fc "tail", at the elbow between its CH2 and CH3 domains, not the arms that bind the drug target. That is why one resin can capture almost any IgG. Contact residues are computed live here as Fc residues with an atom within 4.0 Å of protein A.

Assumptions and limits

One binding step, one rate. Real uptake has film diffusion, pore diffusion and binding in series. The lumped linear-driving-force (LDF) model squeezes them into one rate constant k. It is a common teaching and screening model, not a full general-rate model.

Fitted once. De was fitted to a single published point (MabSelect SuRe, 6 min). The model is then checked against a second point it was not fitted to. Far from 2.4–6 min the curve is an extrapolation, and the page flags that.

Pure, single antibody. No host-cell proteins, no competition, constant pH and salt during loading. Elution (low pH) is described but not simulated yet.

Bed height. At a fixed residence time and Péclet number, bed height cancels out of this model, so there is no bed-height slider. Real columns show small height effects through dispersion and pressure.

Numerics. 100 finite-volume cells. A test checks that doubling the grid changes DBC10 by under 1%, and that injected antibody equals what left, what is bound and what is in the liquid, to within 0.5%.

Why low pH lets the antibody go

Histidine side chains have a pKa near 6. At loading pH (about 7.4) they are mostly neutral. In the elution buffer (pH 3–4, per the MabSelect SuRe data file) they pick up a proton and become positively charged, which weakens the contact and releases the antibody. Turn on "Highlight histidines" to see which ones sit in the contact surface in 1FC2.

Words used on this page
Capture step
The first purification column after harvest. It pulls the antibody out of a dilute, dirty broth.
Protein A
A bacterial protein that binds the Fc part of IgG. It is attached to porous beads to make the resin.
Fc
The constant "stem" of an antibody. The same in most IgG drugs, unlike the target-binding arms (Fab).
Kd
Dissociation constant: the free antibody concentration at which half the binding sites are filled. Smaller means tighter binding.
Isotherm
A curve of how much binds (q) against free concentration (C) once everything has settled, at one temperature.
Breakthrough
The moment antibody starts appearing in the column outlet because the beads near the outlet are full.
DBC10
Dynamic binding capacity: mg of antibody loaded per mL resin when the outlet reaches 10% of the feed concentration.
Residence time
Bed volume divided by flow rate. Longer means slower flow and more time for antibody to diffuse into the beads.
Column volume (CV)
One bed volume of liquid. Loading is often counted in CVs.
LDF
Linear driving force: uptake rate proportional to how far the bead is from being full.
